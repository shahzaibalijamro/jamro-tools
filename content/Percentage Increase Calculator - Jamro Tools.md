## **What Is Percentage Increase?**

Percentage increase shows how much a value has increased compared with its original value.

For example, if a price increases from **$100 to $120**, the increase is **$20**. Compared with the original price, this represents a **20% increase**.

Percentage increase is commonly used to measure:

* Price increases  
* Salary increases  
* Revenue growth  
* Sales growth  
* Population growth  
* Investment growth  
* Website traffic growth  
* Business performance

---

## **Percentage Increase Formula**

The percentage increase formula is:

**Percentage Increase \= ((New Value − Original Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100**

### **Formula Breakdown**

New Value − Original Value \= Increase

Increase ÷ Original Value \= Decimal Change

Decimal Change × 100 \= Percentage Increase

For example, to calculate the percentage increase from **200 to 250**:

250 − 200 \= 50

50 ÷ 200 \= 0.25

0.25 × 100 \= 25%

Therefore, the percentage increase is **25%**.

## **How to Calculate Percentage Increase**

You can calculate percentage increase between two numbers in three simple steps.

### **Step 1: Find the Difference**

Subtract the original value from the new value.

**New Value − Original Value**

### **Step 2: Divide by the Original Value**

Divide the increase by the original value.

**Increase ÷ Original Value**

### **Step 3: Multiply by 100**

Multiply the result by 100 to convert it into a percentage.

**Decimal × 100 \= Percentage Increase**

### **Example**

Calculate the percentage increase from **80 to 100**.

#### **Find the increase**

100 − 80 \= 20

#### **Divide by the original value**

20 ÷ 80 \= 0.25

#### **Convert to a percentage**

0.25 × 100 \= 25%

**The percentage increase from 80 to 100 is 25%.**

## **Percentage Increase Between Two Numbers**

To find the percentage increase between two numbers, you need:

* The **original value**  
* The **new value**

The new value must be greater than the original value for the result to be a percentage increase.

Use this formula:

**((New Value − Original Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100**

For example, the percentage increase from **400 to 500** is:

((500 − 400\) ÷ 400\) × 100

(100 ÷ 400\) × 100

\= 25%

Therefore, the value increased by **25%**.

If the new value is lower than the original value, use a Percentage Decrease Calculator instead.

## **Common Mistakes When Calculating Percentage Increase**

### **Using the New Value as the Base**

The percentage increase should be calculated using the **original value** as the base.

For example, when calculating the increase from 100 to 150:

Correct:

(150 − 100\) ÷ 100 × 100 \= 50%

The original value of **100** is used as the denominator.

---

### **Confusing Percentage Increase With Percentage Difference**

Percentage increase measures change from an original value to a new value.

Percentage difference is generally used to compare two values without treating one as the starting value.

If you need to compare two values rather than measure growth, try the Percentage Difference Calculator.

---

### **Forgetting to Multiply by 100**

After dividing the increase by the original value, multiply the result by 100 to convert it into a percentage.

For example:

0.25 × 100 \= 25%
